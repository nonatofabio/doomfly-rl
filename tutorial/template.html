DoomFly-RL: a fruit-fly brain, wired exactly as nature left it, learns to play Doom.

This is the Doom analogue of chessfly. We take a real Drosophila connectome — every neuron, every synapse, sign and strength — freeze the wiring, and let gradient descent tune only per-synapse gains and per-neuron homeostasis. Pixels go in through the fly's visual sensory neurons; actions come out of its central, descending and motor neurons. Every number on this page was computed from the connectome files that train the model.

1What a connectome is

A connectome is a wiring diagram of a nervous system reconstructed from electron microscopy: a directed graph whose nodes are neurons and whose edges are synaptic connections, each carrying a synapse count and a neurotransmitter-derived sign (excitatory or inhibitory). 2The wiring, in numbers

Real brains are sparse and heavy-tailed. Most neurons talk to a few dozen partners; a handful of hubs talk to thousands. Most synaptic connections are a single synapse; a few are hundreds strong. The network keeps both properties because we never add or remove an edge.

3Two backbones, one model

The default backbone reproduces chessfly's recipe on the FlyWire FAFB v783 whole-brain release. The second is the MaleCNS 49k subset published on Hugging Face; it contains no optic lobe, so its "visual inputs" are the visual-projection and sensory neurons of the central brain instead of photoreceptor-side cells. Both are trained side by side as an ablation of how much of the fly is needed.

4How pixels become actions

The dynamics, one step

Only three things are learned inside the brain: a scalar log-gain per synapse (the sign and the synapse count are fixed biology), and a per-neuron, per-step scale and shift on top of a running batch-normalisation of presynaptic drive — a cheap stand-in for homeostatic plasticity, and the same trick chessfly uses to keep 100k+ ReLU units from exploding or dying. Everything else (the convolutional stem that turns 4 stacked 72×96 frames into currents, the decoder, the two heads) is ordinary and small.

5The Doom side: 22 actions, 5 scenarios, legal-move masking

Chess has a 1968-move vocabulary with a legal-move mask per position. Doom's analogue is a global vocabulary of 22 button combinations with a per-scenario legality mask: basic may only strafe and shoot, deadly_corridor may do almost everything. The policy head always emits 22 logits; illegal ones are masked to −10⁴ before the softmax.

6Training: teacher, rollouts, distillation

Chessfly distils Stockfish-labelled positions. DoomFly has no Stockfish, so it makes its own teacher first. Everything runs unattended on one g6e.12xlarge (4× NVIDIA L40S, 48 GB each) in AWS us-west-2, launched by a CDK stack with no inbound ports (SSM only), logs mirrored to S3 every minute, and the box stops itself when done.

Bootstrap. Pull code + both connectome files from S3, reuse the Deep Learning AMI's PyTorch venv, install ViZDoom / gymnasium / SB3.
1
Teachers. Five PPO agents (SB3 CnnPolicy, 8 parallel envs each), one per scenario, one per GPU round-robin. These are the "Stockfish" of each map.
2
Rollouts. Each teacher plays its scenario ε-greedily (ε = 0.1) for 300k frames. We store the frame stack, the teacher's full action distribution (soft target), the discounted return, and the legality mask, as .npz shards.
3
Distillation into the fly. Two FlyNets train concurrently — FlyWire 783 on GPU 0, MaleCNS 49k on GPU 1 — with soft-target cross-entropy on the policy and a 64-bin categorical cross-entropy on the value (the chessfly/MuZero trick). Three AdamW groups: synaptic log-gains (lr 3e-3), homeostasis, and the rest (lr 3e-4); warm-up + cosine; bf16. Every few thousand steps the fly plays all five scenarios live and saves a GIF of its best episode.
4
Wrap up. Sync checkpoints, eval logs and GIFs to S3, then shutdown -h now.
5
Optional: fine-tune the fly on its own play (GRPO). doomfly/grpo.py takes the distilled checkpoint, plays each scenario in seed-matched groups, and pushes the policy toward the actions that beat the group's mean return — no value network, no teacher, a KL leash to the distilled policy. This is the step that can let the fly surpass its teacher (§7).

Soft targets carry the teacher's uncertainty, which matters when several actions are near-equivalent.

Value loss

Lv = CE(bin(Gt), vfly) over 64 bins
Return normalised per scenario to [0,1], then classified rather than regressed — stabler with heavy-tailed rewards.

7The three algorithms, side by side

Three optimisers touch this project and they answer three different questions. PPO asks "which actions raise the return?" and needs a learned critic to answer it. Distillation asks "what would the teacher do here?" and needs no reward at all. GRPO asks PPO's question again, but replaces the critic with sibling episodes that started from the same seed — the trick behind DeepSeek-R1's reasoning training, applied to a 138k-neuron fly.

1 · PPO (teacher, SB3)

rt = πθ(at|st) / πold(at|st)
L = −E[min(rtÂt, clip(rt,1±ε)Ât)] + cv(V−G)² − cHH
Ât from GAE(λ=0.95) on a learned value head V(s). Clip ε = 0.1, 8 envs × 128 steps per update. Reward pre-scaled ×0.01 on the ±100-reward maps — without it the teacher collapsed to one action.

2 · Distillation (fly ← teacher)

L = −Σa legal pT(a) log pfly(a) + CE(bin(Gt), vfly)
Supervised, off-policy, no environment in the loop. Learns from 1.5M stored frames; the fly's own mistakes never influence what it sees. Ceiling = the teacher.

Why not run GRPO from scratch? A randomly-initialised fly wins ≈0 episodes in deadly_corridor, so every group has zero return variance and zero gradient. Distillation first gives GRPO a policy that sometimes succeeds, which is all a group-relative method needs. Why not plain PPO on the fly? A second 51M-parameter value head doubles memory and, at this scale, learns slower than the policy it is meant to guide; the group baseline is free.

8The data

Every training example is a stack of the last four grayscale frames, area-averaged from ViZDoom's 320×240 render to . Frame-skip 4 means one decision per 4 game tics (≈ 8.75 decisions/s). Each scenario contributes the same number of frames, recorded by its own teacher with ε = random actions so the fly also sees how to recover from mistakes.

One rollout shard (shard_XXXX.npz)

Per-scenario training recipe

Two things we learned the hard way. (1) basic and deadly_corridor pay ±100 per event; SB3 PPO with clip 0.1 saw those as saturating and settled on a single action. Scaling rewards by 0.01 before the learner (Sample Factory does the same) fixed it. (2) deadly_corridor at the default skill 5 is nearly unwinnable for an early agent, so we add health/kill/ammo delta shaping and drop to skill 3 for the teacher; the fly is evaluated on the unshaped reward.
